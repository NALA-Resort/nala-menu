# Nala app: the chef's sheet

Your evening, in order. Four steps, and the reason for each.

---

## 1. Publish the menu

You do this in the menu chat, not in the app: send the photo, confirm the four
courses, and it goes live.

Publishing writes the menu to the website itself rather than into the app's
database. That is why it appears on the guest's phone instantly, and why the
manager is only told about it once somebody has a board open.

**Publish before service, not after.** Guests answering their invitation
before the menu exists are asked to acknowledge that they are agreeing to a
menu they cannot see. The earlier it goes up, the fewer of those there are.

---

## 2. Tag the dietaries

Straight after publishing, open **Menu Dietaries** from the link the chat
gives you.

Tick, for each dish, what it does **not** suit. That is the only question the
page asks.

**Why it matters more than it looks.** Those ticks are what make the guest
page ask for a note. A guest who picks "nut allergy" against an untagged menu
is simply recorded as having a nut allergy. Against a tagged menu they are
asked what it means for them, and you get the answer in the afternoon instead
of at service.

**If there is nothing to tick, no menu is published for today.** The page
builds its rows from tonight's menu. That is not a fault, it is the order of
operations: publish first, tag second.

**The dietary list itself** is yours to manage on the same page. Add one and
choose whether it is common (always offered) or just for this menu. Archiving
one hides it from guests without deleting the history.

If the page says it could not read the list, it will refuse to save. That is
on purpose: saving would replace every dietary you have ever added with the
eight built-in ones.

---

## 3. Read the board

**Reservations** shows who is eating and how many. You can see it and print
from it; you cannot edit a booking. That is reception's job and two editors
for one record is how they drift apart.

The speech bubble beside a villa carries the dietaries and notes. Two
headings, and the difference is the point:

- *Tonight's dining notes* - what they said for tonight.
- *Previous dining notes* - what they said on an earlier night of the same
  stay. **Do not cook to these without asking.** A guest's dietary is an
  answer to one night's invitation, not a standing instruction.

A red bar down the side of a booking means their dietary clashes with a dish
you tagged. That is the tagging in step 2 doing its work.

---

## 4. Print the sheet

**Reservations Sheet** is the paper version, laid out for the pass.

**Printable Menu** produces one A4 landscape sheet holding two menus, to be
cut down the middle.

---

## What the app will never do

It will not reword a dish, invent one, or change your menu. If something on a
menu photo reads as an allergen risk, the chat raises it once and then defers
to you.
